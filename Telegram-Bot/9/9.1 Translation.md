**Introduction**

At the beginning of the course, we briefly touched upon the topic of asynchronous programming. Now, let’s examine it in more detail.

First, let’s clarify what synchronous and asynchronous programming are.

1.  **Synchronous Program:** Sends a request and waits for the result of that request without performing any other useful work. The program can only continue execution after receiving a response from the external server, as its subsequent actions depend on this data.
2.  **Asynchronous Application:** Also sends a request to the server, but unlike the synchronous approach, it does not halt execution while waiting for the response. Instead, it continues performing other tasks that do not depend on the result of the request. Once the response arrives, the program returns to process it.

**Asynchrony and Multithreading**

The concepts of asynchrony and multithreading are often confused. Let’s analyze their fundamental differences.

Asynchrony can be compared to a simple everyday situation. Suppose we want to have tea with sandwiches. Obviously, we would first put the kettle on to heat up, and while it boils the water, we would prepare our food. When the kettle boils, we return to it and pour the tea. In this example, asynchrony is demonstrated by the fact that you do not waste time waiting for the water to boil and simultaneously occupy yourself with preparing food. This is an interpretation of **asynchronous** programming in our daily lives.

Now, let’s consider the same breakfast preparation, but this time we have an assistant—a second person. We divide the labor: one person handles making the tea, while the other prepares the sandwiches. The time spent will be comparable to the first scenario, but the second situation demonstrates **multithreaded** programming.

The difference between these scenarios lies in the fact that the second case utilizes additional resources—the second person. Executing multithreaded code involves **greater** resource consumption by the computer. Essentially, two programs are running instead of one.

Therefore, asynchrony is **not** multithreading, and vice versa. It is possible to create an asynchronous single-threaded program or a synchronous single-threaded program; these two characteristics are independent of each other.
